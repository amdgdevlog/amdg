Zero Unplanned Downtime: Heavy Machinery Predictive Maintenance

Connecting telematics from heavy machinery directly into cloud-native field service workflows to detect mechanical anomalies and fix equipment before a breakdown occurs.

On a busy commercial construction site, heavy machinery is the heartbeat of production. When a 50-ton excavator suffers a sudden hydraulic failure or a tower crane's motor overheats, the impact ripples across the entire jobsite. Earthmoving halts, sub-trades are idle, project schedules slip, and liquidated damages begin to accumulate—costing general contractors thousands of dollars per hour in unplanned downtime.

Traditionally, equipment maintenance operates on two flawed models: reactive repairs (fixing machines only after they break down on site) or calendar-based preventive maintenance (servicing machines every 500 hours, regardless of actual wear and tear).

To maximize fleet availability and eliminate jobsite disruptions, equipment managers are transitioning to condition-based predictive maintenance. By connecting telematics from heavy machinery directly into cloud-native field service workflows, fleet operators can detect subtle mechanical anomalies and fix equipment before a breakdown occurs.

The Problem: Blind Spots, Emergency Repairs, & Inefficient Dispatching

Managing heavy equipment across dispersed job sites presents severe operational challenges for fleet directors and field service mechanics:

  1. Undetected Mechanical Degradation: Fleet managers often have no visibility into minor mechanical issues—such as thermal spikes, micro-vibrations, or pressure drops—until a catastrophic failure shuts down the machine.
  2. High-Cost Emergency Field Repairs: Unplanned breakdowns require emergency dispatches. Mechanics often arrive at remote sites without the required replacement parts or specialized diagnostic tools, resulting in multiple trips and extended downtime.
  3. Wasted Maintenance Capital: Calendar-based servicing frequently leads to premature parts replacements or unnecessary maintenance on low-use equipment, inflating fleet maintenance budgets.

The Solution: Telematics-Driven Action via AWS IoT Core & Salesforce Field Service

By combining AWS IoT Core for real-time equipment telemetry with Salesforce Field Service for intelligent workforce management, contractors create an automated, end-to-end maintenance loop. Equipment issues automatically diagnose themselves and schedule their own repairs.

How the Architecture Works

  1. Telemetry Streaming via AWS IoT Core: Heavy machinery (excavators, dozers, cranes, generators) streams CAN bus telematics data—including engine temperature, oil pressure, vibration metrics, and operating hours—to AWS IoT Core over secure cellular protocols.
  2. Real-Time Anomaly Detection: AWS IoT SiteWise processes the incoming telemetry streams against machine learning baselines. If a hydraulic pump exhibits abnormal vibration patterns or elevated operating temperatures indicative of imminent failure, AWS triggers an automated alert.
  3. Automated Work Order & Parts Allocation: An AWS Lambda payload immediately creates a critical Work Order in Salesforce Field Service. Salesforce automatically cross-references the specific equipment asset history, identifies the exact failing component, and checks local warehouse inventory for necessary replacement parts.
  4. Intelligent Mobile Dispatch: Salesforce Field Service uses AI-powered scheduling rules to automatically assign the service job to the nearest mobile technician who possesses both the required certification and the correct replacement parts on their truck.
  5. Offline Field Execution: The technician receives step-by-step repair instructions and schematic diagrams on the Salesforce Field Service Mobile App, which functions seamlessly even in low-connectivity rural job sites.
